# `self-description/`

Two templates, for two different authors, both self-attested and unverified. Publishing establishes only what an organization says about itself — nothing more. Nothing submitted here is reviewed, scored, or fact-checked by this repo.

## `VENDOR_OPTION_TEMPLATE.md` — for sellers

One block per option sold, not one per organization. A vendor with three products fills this out three times. Use it to state, in your own words, what a specific option does — not what your company does in general.

## `ENVIRONMENT_ACCESS_TEMPLATE.md` — for operators

For operators of environments with no published access regime: the `access-reference/` record's shape, offered to the operator to complete about their own environment. Same sections, same discipline, with the operator as the source rather than a documentation set.

> Complete this if you operate a game or virtual environment and want to state, in one place, what a third party building or activating in it can render, instrument, read, and export. Nothing here is submitted to or reviewed by anyone. Publish it wherever you like.

Its purpose is coverage without written-about-you records. It also does what `access-reference/` cannot: it shows a live-service operator what a documented access regime looks like, which is the only realistic route to more of them existing.

### Boundary flag

This template sits closer to a specification than anything else in this repo, and the corpus this repo is a companion to excludes specifications by policy. It stays in scope on a narrow reading: it asks what an environment makes available, in the operator's own words, with no thresholds, no evaluation logic, no pass conditions, and no statement of what a good answer contains. If a future revision adds any of those, it has become a specification and belongs out of this repo — flag it in review rather than merging it.

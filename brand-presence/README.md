# `brand-presence/`

An environment-level record of demonstrated precedent: which game and virtual environments have carried third-party brand presence at least once.

## What an entry claims

That an environment has carried brand presence placed by a party other than its operator, for that party's own marketing purposes, with the operator's sanction, on at least one occasion. Each entry cites public announcements as existence proof.

**That is the entire claim.** An entry establishes nothing about the environment's current openness to third parties, the terms on which access was granted, the scale or form of what ran, whether it can be repeated, or how it performed. Absence of an entry establishes nothing at all — most environments that have carried brand presence are not listed here, and never will be.

## Why it exists

`access-reference/` records only environments that publish a standing third-party access regime, which is a small set. For everything else, a planner starts from zero: no documentation, no rate card, no way to know whether a door exists at all. An entry here converts *undocumented and unknowable* into *undocumented but demonstrably granted at least once*. That is a small fact, and it is the difference between a cold approach and an informed one.

## Scope

**In scope:** presence placed by a non-operator party, for that party's own marketing, sanctioned by the operator, rendered inside the environment or unlocked into it.

**Out of scope:**

- **Entertainment-IP collaborations** — a game licensing a film, music, or character property as content. These look identical from outside; the test is whether the presence exists to market the third party.
- **Platform advertising products** — presence served through an operator's own ad system is a published access regime and belongs in `access-reference/`.
- **Operator self-promotion, and player-made brand content the operator did not sanction.**
- **Broadcast, tournament, and creator surfaces** around a game rather than inside it.
- **Environments that already have a record in `access-reference/records/`** — where a regime is published, the door and its terms are documented and precedent adds nothing.

## What is not recorded, deliberately

**Campaigns.** The unit is the environment, not the activation. A campaign record has a hole in it shaped like an outcome — it says a thing happened, and every reader immediately wants to know how it went. There is no structural defence against that, only discipline, and discipline is what fails at scale. An environment-level claim has no hole: the fact is the whole fact.

**Outcomes, results, metrics, or assessments of any kind.** Not withheld pending review — not produced. Activation announcements are promotional material written by interested parties. They are adequate evidence that something happened and evidence of nothing else, and this directory asks nothing else of them.

**Completeness.** No claim is made that this list is exhaustive, and it never will be. Coverage follows announcement volume as much as it follows brand presence, which is a reason to read absence as silence rather than as information.

## Sources

Entries cite public announcements. Operator or brand first-party announcements are preferred; trade press is acceptable. Where both exist, cite the first-party source first. Entries carry between one and three citations — enough to establish the fact, not enough to become a campaign summary.

## Maintenance

Entries record historical facts and do not go stale the way access records do, so there is no review cadence. The failure mode is link rot: citations are checked annually, and a dead link is replaced or supplemented rather than the entry being removed.

Delisting is honored on request from the operator, without argument and without a stated reason, on the same terms as the vendor census.

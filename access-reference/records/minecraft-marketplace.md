# Minecraft Marketplace Partner Program — Bedrock Add-On Content

- **`regime_id`:** REGIME/minecraft-marketplace
- **`last_reviewed`:** 2026-08-22
- **`next_review`:** 2026-11-20

## Scope of this record

Building Bedrock Add-On content (resource packs, behavior packs, skin packs, worlds) requires no enrollment at all: Mojang's building tools, JSON/scripting documentation, and the in-game Editor are freely available to any owner of Minecraft: Bedrock Edition. [4][8][9] Enrollment is required only to reach players through the in-game Marketplace storefront. That enrollment runs through the Minecraft Partner Program: a creator or studio applies at `minecraft.net/partner` with a portfolio of "high quality, original content... shared with the Minecraft community" (the page names maps, skin packs, texture packs, hosted servers, add-ons, and Realms content as examples) and is expected to "operate like a business," submitting key art, screenshots, trailers, and descriptions as part of the application. [1] As of retrieval, the official partner page states that direct applications are not being accepted ("Even if we aren't accepting applications at this time there are still other ways to get involved with the partner program") and instead names three publishing studios — Pathway Studios, Waypoint Studios, and Blocklab Studios — as an alternate route to market. [1] Whether working through one of these publishers constitutes enrollment under the same Partner Program terms, or a separate private arrangement between creator and publisher outside Mojang/Microsoft's published terms, is not stated and is recorded in Open Questions.

The base platform is governed by the Minecraft End User License Agreement (EULA) and the Minecraft Usage Guidelines, both of which explicitly exclude Minecraft Education (governed instead by a separate group/organizational agreement with the purchasing school) from their scope. [2][3] Partners additionally sit under a separate Minecraft Marketplace partner agreement, referenced from a Microsoft partner-portal URL (`partner.microsoft.com/.../minecraftagreement.html`); that page returned "Access restricted" at retrieval — it requires an authenticated partner sign-in, and its text (term length, termination, revenue share, and other contractual specifics) could not be read for this record. [11] No official Minecraft- or Mojang-published documentation describing a Marketplace Partner account's internal role/permission structure was located; the role model that the individuation rule turns on is therefore substantially undocumented outside that access gate, and is treated as an open question rather than assumed.

This record does **not** cover the following adjacent, differently-governed activity on the same environment:

- **Java Edition modding and Java/Bedrock server hosting.** These operate under the general, standing permission granted to any player by the EULA and by the Usage Guidelines' "Servers and hosting" and "Extended functionality and modifications" sections — rules anyone may follow without application, acceptance, or any named role. [2][3] This satisfies none of the regime definition's enrollment-path or role-model elements and is not a regime for this repo's purposes.
- **Minecraft Education.** Its own EULA carve-out places it under a separate group/organizational agreement. [2] Its Marketplace-adjacent "Add-Ons Collection" is a curated set of Mojang-selected lesson content, not an open third-party submission surface. [12] Its partnerships page routes commercial or content-development proposals to case-by-case evaluation ("submit a partnership proposal for further evaluation") or back to the general Minecraft Creator community rather than to any separate standing enrollment path of its own. [13] It is negotiated access, not a published standing regime, and is not recorded here.
- **The Minecraft Affiliate Program.** A referral/commission arrangement for driving purchases; affiliates place tracking links, they do not build inside the environment, so it fails the regime definition's first element (a build surface) and is not covered.
- **Minecraft Realms.** A personal/friends hosting service. The EULA expressly forbids selling, leasing, renting, or otherwise commercially dealing in access to a Realm. [2] It is not a third-party build-for-others regime and is not covered here.

## What a third party can render

Any creator with Minecraft: Bedrock Edition can build Add-On content using Mojang's own in-game **Editor** — a multiblock select/clone/move/shape building tool launched from the Bedrock Edition Creator Tools tab of the Minecraft Launcher — plus **Blockbench**, a free modeling/animation tool for custom entities, blocks, and items that Mojang's own creator-tools page explicitly labels as "provided by external, third-party contributors and is not a Mojang or Microsoft offering." [9] Mojang separately publishes official Blockbench plugins (Entity Wizard, Item Wizard, Block Wizard) for rapid, template-based creation of mobs, items, and blocks. [4][9] **Chunker**, a free open-source tool converting world saves between Bedrock and Java formats, is likewise explicitly flagged as a community tool, "not a Mojang or Microsoft offering." [9] Content is authored as JSON-based block/entity/item/recipe/particle definitions, optionally extended with JavaScript/TypeScript scripting through the `@minecraft/server` module and related modules, all documented in Microsoft's Bedrock Edition Creator Documentation and its API reference. [4][5]

None of this building activity requires Partner Program enrollment — a creator can build and use Add-Ons privately without ever applying. Reaching players through the in-game **Marketplace** storefront specifically requires Partner Program acceptance (directly, or via one of the named publisher studios). [1] Published content is organized into storefront categories including Skin Packs, Add-Ons, and Texture Packs, [1] and is purchased by players using **Minecoins**, Minecraft's in-game currency. [1] Every new Marketplace submission is stated to be reviewed by Mojang/Microsoft "for quality and suitability" before being made available to players. [1] The official partner page names four example partner studios (Noxcrew, CrackedCubes, Ariacreations, King Cube) as illustrations of accepted partners, without stating current acceptance criteria beyond the qualitative "Proven Experience / A Love For Minecraft / A Drive to Create / A Business Mindset" framing. [1]

## What behavior the regime lets a third party instrument

**Third-party-defined instrumentation.** No official documentation located for this record describes a creator-facing API or pipeline for logging custom analytics or telemetry events about player behavior inside Marketplace-distributed content (worlds or Add-Ons, which run client-side on a player's device). The one scripting module offering outbound network capability, `@minecraft/server-net` (HTTP client and WebSocket classes), is explicitly documented as usable "only... on Bedrock Dedicated Server" and stated to not function "within the Minecraft game client or within Minecraft Realms" — the two contexts in which Marketplace-purchased content actually runs — and is additionally marked "still in pre-release. It may change or it may be removed in future releases." [6] A full search of the indexed Bedrock Creator Documentation table of contents (2,887 indexed pages) found no page or module under "analytics," "telemetry," "insight," or "engagement." [7] Creators can maintain in-world state through general scripting constructs — scoreboards, entity/world properties, script variables — but the documentation reviewed does not describe any built-in path for that state to leave the world or reach an external dashboard. [4][5]

**Operator-provided metrics.** No official Mojang/Microsoft page describing a creator-facing sales, download, or engagement dashboard for Marketplace partners was located, despite searching minecraft.net's partner and creator pages, the full Bedrock Creator Documentation index, and Minecraft's own news article specifically aimed at prospective Marketplace creators. [1][4][7][8][9][10] This is recorded as an open question, not as an absence of the capability: the Partner Program plainly generates sales requiring some settlement mechanism, but no description of it was found published outside an authenticated partner session.

**Available breakdowns/segmentation.** Not documented in any source reachable without a partner account; recorded as unknown.

## What a third party can read and export, and who can read it

**The binding constraint here is an absence of published documentation, not a documented gate.** This record could not locate — after searching minecraft.net's partner- and creator-facing pages, the full Bedrock Creator Documentation index, and Microsoft's partner-agreement landing page — any publicly published description of what a Minecraft Marketplace partner can see or export about their own sales, downloads, or player engagement, or of who on a partner's team can see it. The one governing-terms page located that plausibly covers this, the Marketplace partner agreement hosted at `partner.microsoft.com` (URL slug `minecraftagreement.html`), returned "Access restricted" at retrieval: it requires an authenticated partner sign-in, and its content — including any partner-portal reporting or team-permission provisions — was not readable for this record. [11] No Minecraft- or Mojang-branded creator dashboard, payout report, or data-export page was found published outside that gate.

Unlike this repository's Roblox record, which documents a public Creator Dashboard with an explicit, role-gated team-access model (Edit-only vs. Group-role-based analytics/finance separation), this record cannot state whether an analogous role split — e.g., financial/payout visibility versus content-management-only access — exists for Minecraft Marketplace partner teams. That is an open question below, not a documented absence: the platform may well have such a system behind the partner-account sign-in this record could not pass, but nothing publicly published confirms or describes it.

## Open questions

- The precise, quantitative eligibility criteria for Partner Program enrollment (minimum age, business-entity registration requirement, minimum portfolio size, application review timeline). The official partner page states qualitative criteria only (proven experience/portfolio, "a business mindset," submission of key art/screenshots/trailers/descriptions); numeric figures reported elsewhere were found only in unofficial third-party guides and are not confirmed by any source fetched for this record.
- Whether the Partner Program is currently accepting direct applications at all: the official page states "Even if we aren't accepting applications at this time..." as of retrieval, without a visible date on that statement, and directs prospective creators to three named publisher studios instead. Current direct-application status beyond the retrieval date is unknown.
- The content and precise obligations of the governing Minecraft Marketplace Partner Agreement. Its landing page on `partner.microsoft.com` returned "Access restricted" at retrieval, so term length, termination rights, revenue share, payout cadence/threshold, and other contractual specifics could not be sourced to a document actually read for this record.
- Whether Microsoft Partner Center — the general Microsoft commercial-marketplace portal, whose publicly documented role model includes Owner/Manager/Developer/Financial Contributor tiers for unrelated Microsoft marketplace programs — is in fact the system Minecraft Marketplace partners use for sales reporting and payouts, and if so, whether that general role model actually governs Minecraft Marketplace accounts specifically. No official Minecraft- or Mojang-published documentation confirming this integration was located.
- Whether a Marketplace Partner account supports multiple team members with differentiated, role-gated access (e.g., sales/financial data vs. content-management-only), analogous to the role-gating this repository's Roblox record documents. No official documentation describing a Minecraft Marketplace-specific team/role/permission system was found.
- Whether creators have any documented mechanism to instrument or log custom behavioral/telemetry events about their own Marketplace-distributed content and have that data reach any read surface. The only network-capable scripting module found (`@minecraft/server-net`) is explicitly restricted to Bedrock Dedicated Server deployments, is stated not to function in the game client or on Realms, and is documented as pre-release.
- What export formats, cadence, and read surfaces (if any) exist for a partner to see their own sales/download/engagement data. No official page describing a creator dashboard, sales report, or export mechanism was found.
- Whether publishing through one of the named publisher studios (Pathway Studios, Waypoint Studios, Blocklab Studios) constitutes enrollment under the same Partner Program terms and role model documented (or undocumented) here, or is instead a private business relationship between creator and publisher governed by terms Mojang/Microsoft does not publish at all — which would make it a separate, negotiated arrangement rather than part of this regime.
- The precise legal name of the governing partner agreement. The only located textual trace is the URL slug `minecraftagreement.html` on Microsoft's partner portal; the page itself was inaccessible.
- The "Becoming a Minecraft Marketplace Partner" help-center article (`help.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/360058530652`) could not be retrieved as readable content — the page is rendered client-side and returned only navigation chrome to this record's fetch tooling. Any additional enrollment detail it may contain is unconfirmed and not used here.
